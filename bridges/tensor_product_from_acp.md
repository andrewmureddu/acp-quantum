# Tensor-Product Composition from ACP Independent-Composition Axioms

*Status: exploratory bridge note; the main theorem is **proved** conditional on
local complex Hilbert spaces together with ACP-motivated axioms for independent
composition. A first-principles derivation of those local Hilbert spaces from
ACP alone remains open.*

## 1. Thesis

The previous two quantum-foundations bridge notes established:

1. `bridges/born_rule_from_acp.md`: on a Hilbert branch decomposition,
   ACP-style conservation and coarse-graining force branch weights
   \(W(v)=\|v\|^2\).
2. `bridges/unitary_evolution_from_acp.md`: on Hilbert ray space,
   ACP-style continuous mechanism-preserving evolution is unitary.

The next structural question is composition.

Why does a pair of independent quantum subsystems combine by the tensor-product
rule rather than by direct sum or some other composition law?

This note gives the clean conditional answer:

> if independent subsystems compose through a bilinear branch-composition map,
> if independent branch weights multiply, and if composite prediction states are
> generated by superposing such independent products, then the composite
> prediction space is unitarily equivalent to the tensor product
> \(\mathcal H_A\otimes\mathcal H_B\).

So the tensor product is the unique linear norm-compatible realization of ACP
independent composition once local Hilbert branch spaces are granted.

## 2. Setup

Let subsystem \(A\) have complex Hilbert space \(\mathcal H_A\), and
subsystem \(B\) have complex Hilbert space \(\mathcal H_B\).

For branch vectors \(v\in\mathcal H_A\) and \(w\in\mathcal H_B\), the Born
weights are

$$
W_A(v)=\|v\|^2,
\qquad
W_B(w)=\|w\|^2 .
$$

We seek a composite prediction space \(\mathcal H_{AB}\) together with a map

$$
\beta:\mathcal H_A\times\mathcal H_B\to \mathcal H_{AB}
$$

that represents independent composition.

## 3. ACP-Motivated Composition Axioms

### Axiom TP-1 (Independent preparation)

If subsystem \(A\) is prepared in branch vector \(v\) and subsystem \(B\) in
branch vector \(w\), there exists a composite branch vector \(\beta(v,w)\)
representing that independent joint preparation.

Interpretation: independent subsystems can be jointly prepared without first
measuring or collapsing one another.

### Axiom TP-2 (Coarse-graining distributivity)

Independent composition respects superposition/coarse-graining in each slot:

$$
\beta(v_1+v_2,w)=\beta(v_1,w)+\beta(v_2,w),
$$

$$
\beta(v,w_1+w_2)=\beta(v,w_1)+\beta(v,w_2),
$$

and similarly for complex scalars,

$$
\beta(\alpha v,w)=\alpha\,\beta(v,w),
\qquad
\beta(v,\gamma w)=\gamma\,\beta(v,w).
$$

So \(\beta\) is bilinear.

Interpretation: if a local branch is itself a coherent sum of alternatives,
composing it with an independent partner must preserve that bookkeeping rather
than collapsing it.

### Axiom TP-3 (Independent branch-weight multiplicativity)

For independent branch vectors,

$$
W_{AB}(\beta(v,w))=W_A(v)\,W_B(w).
$$

Using the Born-weight theorem, this is

$$
\|\beta(v,w)\|^2=\|v\|^2\|w\|^2 .
$$

Interpretation: if two systems are independently prepared, their betting powers
multiply.

### Axiom TP-4 (Span generation)

The composite prediction space is generated by independent products:

$$
\mathcal H_{AB}=\overline{\mathrm{span}}\{\beta(v,w):v\in\mathcal H_A,\,
w\in\mathcal H_B\}.
$$

In finite dimensions, this is simply span equality.

Interpretation: entangled states are not primitive extra objects grafted onto
the theory from outside; they arise as superpositions of independently
generated product branches.

## 4. Main Theorem

### Theorem 4.1 (Tensor-product realization)

Under Axioms TP-1 through TP-4, there exists a unique linear isometry

$$
T:\mathcal H_A\otimes\mathcal H_B\to\mathcal H_{AB}
$$

such that

$$
T(v\otimes w)=\beta(v,w)
$$

for all \(v\in\mathcal H_A\), \(w\in\mathcal H_B\).

If TP-4 holds, \(T\) is onto, hence unitary. Therefore

$$
\mathcal H_{AB}\cong \mathcal H_A\otimes\mathcal H_B
$$

up to unitary equivalence.

*Proof.* Because \(\beta\) is bilinear by TP-2, the universal property of the
tensor product yields a unique linear map

$$
T:\mathcal H_A\otimes\mathcal H_B\to\mathcal H_{AB}
$$

such that \(T(v\otimes w)=\beta(v,w)\).

We now show that \(T\) preserves inner products on simple tensors. Let
\(v,v'\in\mathcal H_A\) and \(w,w'\in\mathcal H_B\). By polarization, it is
enough to control norms of simple tensors built from linear combinations.
TP-3 gives

$$
\|T(v\otimes w)\|^2
=
\|\beta(v,w)\|^2
=
\|v\|^2\|w\|^2
=
\|v\otimes w\|^2 .
$$

First fix \(w\). Since \(\beta\) is bilinear,

$$
\beta(v+\lambda v',w)=\beta(v,w)+\lambda\beta(v',w)
$$

for every \(\lambda\in\mathbb C\). Applying TP-3 to \(v+\lambda v'\) and using
the complex polarization identity in the first slot gives

$$
\langle \beta(v,w),\beta(v',w)\rangle
=
\langle v,v'\rangle\,\|w\|^2 .
$$

Now for fixed \(v,v'\), define

$$
B_{v,v'}(w,w')
:=
\langle \beta(v,w),\beta(v',w')\rangle .
$$

By bilinearity of \(\beta\), \(B_{v,v'}\) is sesquilinear in \((w,w')\), and
its diagonal satisfies

$$
B_{v,v'}(w,w)=\langle v,v'\rangle\,\|w\|^2 .
$$

Applying the complex polarization identity in the second slot yields

$$
\langle T(v\otimes w),\,T(v'\otimes w')\rangle
=
\langle v,v'\rangle\langle w,w'\rangle .
$$

So \(T\) is an isometry on the algebraic tensor product, hence extends to an
isometry on the Hilbert completion \(\mathcal H_A\otimes\mathcal H_B\).

Finally, TP-4 says the image of \(T\) spans \(\mathcal H_{AB}\), so the
isometry is surjective. A surjective isometry is unitary. Therefore
\(\mathcal H_{AB}\) is unitarily equivalent to
\(\mathcal H_A\otimes\mathcal H_B\). \(\square\)

### Corollary 4.2 (Product states and entangled states)

The vectors \(\beta(v,w)\) are exactly the product states under the unitary
identification \(T\). Every composite pure state is either:

- a product state \(T(v\otimes w)\), or
- an entangled state given by a nontrivial superposition of such products.

So entanglement is not an additional composition rule. It is the generic
superposition structure on the tensor-product space.

### Corollary 4.3 (Independent unitary evolution factorizes)

Suppose \(A\) and \(B\) evolve independently between measurements via unitary
groups \(U_t^A\) and \(U_t^B\) as established in
`bridges/unitary_evolution_from_acp.md`. Then the induced composite closed
evolution on product states is

$$
T\!\left((U_t^A v)\otimes(U_t^B w)\right)
=
\beta(U_t^A v,U_t^B w).
$$

Under the unitary identification \(T\), this is exactly the tensor-product
unitary

$$
U_t^{AB}=U_t^A\otimes U_t^B
$$

on independent sectors.

## 5. ACP Reading

### 5.1 Why direct sum is wrong for independent coexistence

A direct sum \(\mathcal H_A\oplus\mathcal H_B\) represents an either/or choice:
the system is in \(A\) or in \(B\). That is not independent coexistence.

Independent preparation requires a joint branch carrying both local prediction
structures at once. The tensor product supplies exactly that.

### 5.2 Why multiplicativity is the key invariant

In the Born-weight note, independent composition already satisfied

$$
W(v\otimes w)=W(v)W(w).
$$

The present note reverses the logic:

- multiplicativity is taken as the ACP independent-composition axiom;
- bilinearity turns that axiom into a universal linear map;
- norm multiplicativity then forces the tensor-product norm.

So the tensor product is the unique way to combine independent branch budgets
without violating the local branch-accounting law.

### 5.3 Entanglement as productive surplus

TP-4 says the composite is generated by products, not exhausted by them. Once
the tensor-product space exists, superpositions of product generators create
entangled states. In ACP language, entanglement is additional coordinated
prediction capacity opened by composition, not a violation of independent local
bookkeeping.

## 7. What This Does Not Yet Do

This note does **not** derive local complex Hilbert spaces from ACP alone.
It also still imports:

- bilinearity of the independent-composition map;
- span generation by product branches;
- the local Born-weight geometry on each subsystem.

So the actual theorem is:

> given local Hilbert branch spaces and ACP-style independent-composition
> axioms, the composite branch space is tensor-product up to unitary
> equivalence.
