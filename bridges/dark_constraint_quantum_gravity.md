# Darkness as Constraint: A Quantum-Gravity Bridge Seed

*Status: active boundary-record component of the ACP quantum-gravity derivation
program; conjectural as a quantum-gravity extension; toy optical-inference
simulations added in `simulations/dark_constraint_inference/` and
`simulations/dark_constraint_wave_interference/`.*

## 1. Seed Intuition

Andrew's prompt:

> darkness provides the constraint.
>
> in a room full of mirrors, watching from above, you could watch the light find
> the fastest path. i imagine that there would be competition. competing
> predictions.

The technical reading is:

1. Light does not merely reveal a pre-existing geometry. Light records constrain
   the class of geometries compatible with what is seen and not seen.
2. A dark region is not absence of information. It is a null measurement: a
   constraint on which photon histories, causal paths, and metric configurations
   are compatible with the boundary record.
3. In a mirror room, many candidate optical histories compete. In the
   semiclassical limit, the surviving visible path is the stationary-phase /
   Fermat path. The "fastest path" is the prediction that remains coherent under
   the boundary constraints.
4. In quantum gravity, the same structure should be lifted from competing light
   paths on a fixed geometry to competing geometry-plus-field histories under a
   boundary visibility constraint.

This note turns the image into a first ACP-compatible formal object and a
boundary-record rung in `bridges/quantum_gravity_derivation_program.md`.

## 2. Classical Optical Core

Let \(M\) be a spatial domain with reflecting boundary components
\(\{\Sigma_i\}\). Let \(x_s\) be a source, \(x_o\) an observer, and let
\(\Gamma(x_s,x_o)\) be the set of piecewise smooth paths from source to observer
that satisfy the law of reflection at mirror surfaces.

For a refractive index field \(n(x)\), define the optical action

$$
T[\gamma] = \frac{1}{c}\int_{\gamma} n(x)\, ds .
$$

The classical ray paths are stationary points:

$$
\delta T[\gamma] = 0.
$$

In the mirror room, there may be many stationary paths \(\gamma_k\), with
different reflection sequences and arrival times. The observer does not see
"the" path in advance. The observer sees a boundary record generated by a
competition among candidate paths.

In wave optics, this competition is explicit. The amplitude for detecting light
at \(x_o\) is schematically

$$
\mathcal A(x_o|x_s)
\approx
\sum_{\gamma \in \Gamma(x_s,x_o)}
a_\gamma \exp(i\omega T[\gamma]).
$$

In the high-frequency limit, paths with non-stationary \(T[\gamma]\) cancel by
phase oscillation. The stable visible rays are the stationary-phase survivors.
Thus Fermat's principle is not only a minimization rule; it is a selection rule
over competing predictions.

## 3. Darkness as a Null Constraint

Let \(R_+\) denote positive boundary records: detections, arrival times,
directions, intensities, phases. Let \(R_0\) denote null records: detector
regions and time windows where no photon is observed above threshold.

For a candidate geometry \(g\) and matter configuration \(\phi\), let

$$
P(R_+,R_0|g,\phi)
$$

be the likelihood of the full boundary record. The dark record is informative
whenever

$$
P(R_0|g_1,\phi_1) \ne P(R_0|g_2,\phi_2)
$$

for two candidate histories. Equivalently,

$$
I(G;R_0) > 0,
$$

where \(G\) is a random variable over candidate geometries.

So darkness is a constraint in the literal inferential sense: it removes
candidate geometries whose predicted light transport would have produced
detections in the dark set.

This is the optical analog of syndrome information in the QEC front. A syndrome
need not be a bright positive mark; it can be the structured absence of a mark.

## 4. Lift to Quantum Histories

For quantum fields on a fixed background, the boundary amplitude can be written
schematically as a sum over field histories. For quantum gravity, the metric is
also part of the history:

$$
Z[R_+,R_0]
=
\int_{\mathcal C(R_0)}
\mathcal D g\,\mathcal D\phi\,
\exp\left(\frac{i}{\hbar}S[g,\phi]\right)
\mathcal O_{R_+}[g,\phi].
$$

Here \(\mathcal C(R_0)\) is the constraint class of histories compatible with
the dark record, and \(\mathcal O_{R_+}\) inserts the positive boundary
detections.

The mirror-room competition becomes:

- fixed geometry: competing optical paths \(\gamma\);
- semiclassical gravity: competing null geodesics on \(g\);
- quantum gravity: competing pairs \((g,\phi)\) that generate the same partial
  boundary record.

The classical spacetime seen by an observer is then the stable effective
prediction after incompatible histories destructively interfere or are ruled
out by the combined bright/dark boundary record.

## 5. ACP Translation

The ACP block structure is:

| ACP object | Optical / gravitational reading |
|---|---|
| Boundary variables \(B\) | detector records, mirror boundary data, horizon-accessible observables |
| Internal variables \(I\) | unobserved path segments, inaccessible metric degrees of freedom, dark regions |
| Internal block \(D\) | hidden geometry/path covariance compatible with records |
| Schur complement \(Q/D\) | effective boundary optical/gravitational law after eliminating dark degrees |
| Crystallization | one rigid path/history only; zero residual uncertainty |
| Dissolution | too many unconstrained histories; no stable prediction |
| Productive interval | enough darkness to preserve hidden degrees, enough light/null structure to constrain them |

The key target becomes:

$$
I(\mathrm{geometry\ sector};R_+,R_0) > 0
$$

while

$$
I(\mathrm{interior\ quantum\ state};\mathrm{external\ fragments})
\approx 0
$$

for the degrees of freedom that must remain private for unitary persistence.

This mirrors the active QEC thesis:

$$
I(\mathrm{error};\mathrm{syndrome}) > 0
$$

while

$$
I(\mathrm{logical\ state};\mathrm{environment}) \approx 0.
$$

The quantum-gravity version says: the exterior record may constrain the
geometry, including by structured darkness, without fully leaking the interior
state.

## 6. Horizons as Maximum Dark Constraints

This note connects to `bridges/restraint_power.md`, where Bekenstein bounds and
cosmic censorship are treated as conjectural gravitational coordination floors.
It also connects to `bridges/singularity_inadmissibility.md`, which states the
stronger admissibility criterion: singularities are not physical states; they
are failures of the effective description's boundary/interior decomposition.

An event horizon is not mere absence of observation. It is a stable dark
constraint: a boundary that prevents direct access to interior local degrees of
freedom while preserving exterior predictability through mass, charge, angular
momentum, horizon area, and eventually Hawking radiation.

In ACP terms, the horizon avoids both bad extremes:

- no horizon / naked singularity: the high-curvature region leaks breakdown of
  the effective theory directly into the exterior boundary;
- total darkness with no boundary budget: the exterior receives no usable
  constraint and the interior becomes physically disconnected;
- horizon-bounded darkness: the exterior cannot inspect the interior directly,
  but the horizon supplies a finite coordination budget.

This reframes "darkness provides the constraint" as a gravitational principle:
stable spacetime may require regions whose contents are not directly visible,
provided their boundary darkness is structured enough to constrain exterior
dynamics.

The singularity itself is not the dark constraint. The singularity is the
inadmissible endpoint. The horizon is the candidate repair: it replaces direct
exposure of undefined interior data with a finite, structured exterior boundary
channel.

## 7. Working Conjectures

**Conjecture 1 (Dark-constraint inference).** In any gravitational system with
partial optical access, null records \(R_0\) contribute positive information
about the compatible metric class whenever the candidate metrics induce
different causal visibility relations:

$$
I(G;R_0) > 0.
$$

**Conjecture 2 (Stationary geometry from constrained competition).** A classical
metric \(g_{\mathrm{cl}}\) emerges as a stable prediction when the constrained
history integral \(Z[R_+,R_0]\) is dominated by a stationary-phase class of
geometry-field histories.

**Conjecture 3 (Horizon privacy condition).** A horizon-bounded region is
productive when exterior records carry information about conserved geometric
charges and horizon area while remaining approximately blind to the detailed
interior quantum state:

$$
I(\mathrm{charges,area};R_{\mathrm{ext}}) > 0,
$$

but

$$
I(\mathrm{interior\ microstate};R_{\mathrm{ext}}) \approx 0
$$

until the appropriate decoding time scale.

**Conjecture 4 (Quantum-gravity productive interval).** Quantum gravity is the
theory of the productive interval between unconstrained metric superposition
(dissolution of classical geometry) and fully fixed classical geometry
(crystallization). Light, including darkness-as-null-record, is one boundary
channel by which that interval is selected.

## 8. Minimal Simulation Target

A first toy model does not need full quantum gravity. The minimal executable
version is now in `simulations/dark_constraint_inference/` as an optical inverse
problem:

1. Generate a two-dimensional mirror room with hidden obstacles or refractive
   regions.
2. Sample candidate geometries \(G_k\).
3. Emit rays or wave packets from a source.
4. Record both detections \(R_+\) and dark detector windows \(R_0\).
5. Compute posterior concentration:

$$
P(G_k|R_+,R_0) \propto P(R_+,R_0|G_k)P(G_k).
$$

6. Compare inference using \(R_+\) alone against inference using
   \((R_+,R_0)\).

The target result:

$$
I(G;R_+,R_0) > I(G;R_+)
$$

whenever dark windows are structured by the hidden geometry. This would make
the slogan precise in the weakest possible model: darkness provides constraint
because null records improve geometric inference.

In the first toy implementation, \(R_0\) is operationalized as detector bins
below a calibrated low-light threshold, not as exact zero-photon events. This
keeps the model close to finite-resolution measurement rather than idealized
perfect darkness.

The first ray-count result is positive but limited: structured null records
reduce posterior uncertainty by a mean \(0.6250\) bits and improve the
posterior-mean estimate, while MAP localization is already saturated by the
positive-only record.

## 9. Wave-Interference Upgrade

The ray-blocking model is too close to ordinary occlusion. The next model,
`simulations/dark_constraint_wave_interference/`, tests the same bridge claim
in a phase-sensitive setting.

A point source emits scalar waves in a two-dimensional mirrored room. For a
candidate weak refractive bump \(g\), the detector amplitude is modeled as a
coherent sum over reflected image paths:

$$
\mathcal A(x_o|x_s,g)
=
\sum_{\gamma}
a_\gamma
\exp\!\left(i k S_\gamma[g]\right),
$$

where

$$
S_\gamma[g]
=
\ell_\gamma
+
\int_\gamma \delta n_g(x)\,ds .
$$

Candidate geometries differ by the horizontal position of the weak phase bump.
The observed record is a fringe pattern. Dark bins are now dark because of
destructive interference and phase competition, not because an absorber simply
blocks rays.

The two inference channels are:

$$
R_+ = \{x_i : N_i > N_{\mathrm{dark}}\},
$$

and

$$
(R_+,R_0)=\{(x_i,N_i)\}_{i=1}^m,
\qquad
R_0=\{x_i:N_i\leq N_{\mathrm{dark}}\}.
$$

The benchmark compares posterior entropy:

$$
H(G|R_+)
\quad\text{versus}\quad
H(G|R_+,R_0).
$$

The current default scan gives:

$$
\mathbb E\left[H(G|R_+)-H(G|R_+,R_0)\right]
=0.1194\ \mathrm{bits}.
$$

The gain is positive in every scanned hidden-bump case. MAP localization is
unchanged at the current candidate-grid resolution because the bright-only
fringes already identify the nearest grid point. Thus the wave result should be
read as a posterior-sharpening result: dark fringes add likelihood weight, but
the toy is not yet in a regime where they change the winning geometry class.

## 10. What Is Not Claimed

This note does not derive Einstein gravity, quantize the metric, solve the black
hole information problem, or prove cosmic censorship.

It proposes a bridge variable: structured darkness as a null syndrome for
geometry. The claim is formal enough to simulate in optical models and aligned
enough with existing ACP machinery to connect later to holography, Bekenstein
bounds, gravitational decoherence, and quantum error correction.

The next serious step is not to inflate the claim. It is to strengthen the toy
mirror-room inference model: add moving mirrors or time-dependent obstacles,
then ask whether the same null-record advantage survives when the hidden phase
bump is replaced by a weak metric perturbation or lensing field.
